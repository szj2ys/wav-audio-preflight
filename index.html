WAV Audio Preflight

Inspect the structure and channel samples of a local PCM16 WAV file. Download a factual handoff for a sound volunteer or recording reviewer, without uploading or changing the audio.

These are file and sample measurements, not a sound-quality score. This page does not play or record audio, assess intelligibility, or predict transcript or translation accuracy.

1. Choose a file

Supports little-endian RIFF/WAVE, uncompressed signed 16-bit PCM, one or two channels, 8,000–96,000 Hz, up to 16 MiB (16,777,216 bytes). A filename or MIME type is not used to decide its format.

Files stay in browser memory. No upload, analytics, storage, microphone or playback access. A downloaded report includes the escaped filename and measurements, but no audio or metadata payloads. Share it only with people who need it.

2. Inspect the measurements

3. Download the handoff

The report records the supported format, thresholds, per-channel statistics and their limits. It is JSON, not a spreadsheet or an audio conversion. The original file is never rewritten.

What the numbers mean

  • Frames and duration: one frame contains one signed sample from each channel. Frames = data bytes ÷ block alignment; seconds = frames ÷ sample rate. Channel sample count equals frame count.
  • Peak: maximum absolute sample value ÷ 32,768. Signed values range from −32,768 to +32,767, so the negative endpoint has magnitude 1 and the positive endpoint is about 0.999969482.
  • RMS: square root of the mean of squared samples, divided by 32,768. This is a sample-domain amplitude measure, not calibrated loudness, LUFS or a true-peak measurement.
  • Endpoint samples: counts of −32,768 and +32,767, separately. Their presence alone does not prove clipping or identify its cause. Review the source context in your usual audio workflow.
  • Near-zero samples: count where absolute integer amplitude is ≤32, inclusive (32 ÷ 32,768 = 0.0009765625). This fixed diagnostic threshold is arbitrary, not a silence or intelligibility standard. Exact zeros are counted separately.

A quiet or unlike channel may be intentional. Confirm the intended channel routing, recording length and source context with the person who made the file. These statistics cannot establish equipment quality, hearing safety, correct language, or readiness for a production upload.

Supported bytes and explicit rejection policy
  • The outer header must be RIFF with type WAVE. Its declared size plus 8 must equal the complete file length; trailing or truncated bytes are rejected.
  • Exactly one fmt chunk and one nonempty data chunk, in either order. At most 256 chunks total. Every header and payload must fit, including one padding byte after any odd payload length. The padding value is not interpreted.
  • Format tag 1, signed 16-bit little-endian samples, one or two channels and an integer rate of 8,000–96,000 Hz. The fmt payload must be 16 bytes, or 18 bytes with cbSize = 0. Other format payloads are unsupported by this tool, not necessarily invalid WAV files.
  • Block alignment must equal channels × 2, byte rate must equal sample rate × block alignment, and data bytes must be an exact positive multiple of block alignment. Empty data is rejected.
  • All other chunk payloads are opaque: their outer lengths and padding are checked, their bytes are never displayed or semantically validated. No assumption of a 44-byte header. Compressed/float/extensible, RF64 and big-endian RIFX files are unsupported.

Opening the picker, changing the file or resetting clears previous results and disables the old export. Canceling the picker leaves no report. Older reads cannot replace newer results. Nothing persists after reload.
